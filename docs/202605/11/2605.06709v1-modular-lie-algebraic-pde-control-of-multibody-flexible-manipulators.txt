# Modular Lie Algebraic PDE Control of Multibody Flexible Manipulators 

S. Yaqubi, J. Mattila 

Abstract —This paper addresses the open problem of PDE-based control for flexible multibody robotic systems, present-ing a subsystem-based control framework for serial flexible manipulators with an arbitrary number of links operating in three-dimensional space. The framework builds on a screw-theoretic Lie-algebraic synthesis model in which all dynamic quantities — rigid-body motion, elastic deformation, and inter-link constraint forces — are expressed uniformly as body-fixed twists and dual wrenches within the se (3) structure. A control-lable form of the per-link dynamics is derived by substituting the strain-based deformation PDE into the dynamic equation, eliminating the distributed elastic acceleration term and yielding a model governed solely by the body-fixed twist acceleration and the deformation field. Desired subsystem twist trajectories are generated from the task-space endpoint trajectory via a deflection-compensating inverse kinematics procedure, in which the tip deformation field evaluated at the link endpoint provides a real-time correction to the rigid-body reference. A nominal subsystem-level controller is designed for each link and proven to produce exponential decay of the twist error via a per-subsystem Lyapunov function νi, whose time derivative contains boundary interaction power terms arising from the inter-link wrenches. An adaptive modification is subsequently derived that replaces the exact physical parameters with online estimates governed by a projection-based adaptation law, augmenting each νi with a parameter estimation error term and establishing exponential convergence of both the twist error and the parameter error at the subsystem level. Upon summing over all links, the composite Lyapunov functions V = P

The screw-theoretic geometric struc-ture is therefore not merely a modeling convenience: it is the mechanism that renders the interaction term cancellation exact and makes the stability certificate modular and scalable to chains of arbitrary length. Implementability and physical consistency of the proposed control scheme is numerically investigated in an illustrative example.

Precise trajectory tracking under these conditions is inher-ently challenging: the deformation field interacts nonlinearly with the rigid-body motion, producing underactuation, non-minimum phase characteristics, and spillover instability when finite-dimensional approximations are substituted for the true deformation model in the control design [3], [4]. Two broad strategies dominate the existing literature. The first replaces the distributed deformation by a truncated finite-dimensional ordinary differential equation (ODE), either through assumed modes or finite elements [5]; the resulting controller is tractable but introduces truncation error whose influence on the true closed-loop behavior cannot be formally quantified. The second linearizes the rigid-body kinematics, which sidesteps geometric nonlinearity at the cost of restricting validity to small-displacement operating conditions [6]. The present work takes a different approach: the PDE structure of the defor-mation is carried through the entire control design without spatial discretization, the nonlinear rigid-body dynamics are represented geometrically on se (3) using screw theory, and the stability of the resulting infinite-dimensional closed-loop system is established formally. The theory of PDE-based control for flexible structures is well-established in the single-body setting. Boundary control methods [7], [8] place actuation at the physical endpoints of the PDE domain — typically the base and tip of a flexible link — and construct stability arguments through Lyapunov functionals defined directly on the infinite-dimensional state, bypassing spatial discretization entirely. The resulting guar-antees are mathematically rigorous and free from spillover, but the derivations are strongly tied to the specific system under consideration: the Lyapunov functional, the admissible boundary inputs, and the stability reasoning must each be reconstructed for every new configuration [9]. Extending this program to chains of flexible links compounds the difficulty considerably. The holonomic joints coupling adjacent links introduce constraint forces that enter the boundary conditions of each link’s deformation PDE, and assembling a closed-loop stability certificate for an n-link chain from individual per-link results is a problem with few precedents in the literature. The most substantial contribution toward this objective is due to Zhu [10], who developed a virtual decomposition control framework that achieves subsystem-level stability synthesis for multibody systems. However, in that work and in related

Beyond this, the overwhelming majority of PDE-based flexible manipulator controllers reported in the literature are designed for one or two links specifically [3], [11], and a scalable synthesis procedure applicable to systems with an arbitrary number of flexible links remains an open problem. For rigid multibody systems, the projection from SE(3) to its Lie algebra se (3) via screw-theoretic twist and wrench co-ordinates is well-established as a tool for modular composition of link dynamics [10], [12], [13] and control [14]. Within this framework, inter-link coupling reduces to linear Adjoint transformations, and the forward dynamic map for an n-link chain assembles algebraically rather than through nested non-linear compositions. Extending the same geometric structure to flexible bodies introduces substantial additional complexity: the elastic field must be lifted to a continuous screw field distributed along the body [15], [16], and its coupling to the rigid-body configuration causes the forward dynamic map to take the deeply nested form f1(f2(· · · (fn(·)) · · · )) , where each factor fi incorporates gravitational contributions, trigonometric position dependence, generalized forces, and integrals over shape functions [17], [18]. This nesting obstructs algebraic assembly, prevents straightforward automation for arbitrary n,and destroys the linear operator structure on which systematic control design depends. Representing link velocities as body-fixed twists and internal forces as wrenches in se (3) and 

se ∗(3) recovers this structure: velocity-dependent generalized forces arise naturally from the adjoint action on the body-fixed twist [12], [13], [19], the deformation field is expressed as a body-fixed continuous screw field [9], [16], and inter-link kinematic and dynamic coupling reduces to structured Ad-joint transformations [10], [12] — a property that propagates directly into the model-based control layer. Controlling multibody flexible robots is less investigated than their modeling, and the available research is often de-signed for limited number of links rather than a modular general framework. ODE-based methods derived from the Floating Frame of Reference (FFR) formulation [20], [21] or modal truncation [22] yield finite-dimensional plant models amenable to classical and adaptive control, but at the cost of truncation-induced spillover. Geometrically exact formula-tions [23], [24] and Lie group variational integrators based on Cosserat rod theory [1], [25], [26] capture large deformations with high fidelity, but working directly on the configuration manifold complicates the derivation of control laws and Lya-punov arguments as they would commonly require finite ele-ment solutions rather than modal-based analysis [10]. Screw-theoretic model extensions — including dual screw models with linearized elastic motion [16], finite-element screw-based formulations [27], Lie group formulations for geometrically exact beams [25], Lie algebra integration schemes [26], and screw theoretic synthesis models [28]— demonstrate the po-tential of screw-theoretic representations for dynamics, though their exploitation for systematic PDE-based control synthesis remains an open research direction. Despite these advances, a modular and scalable PDE-based control framework for flexible multibody manipulators within a Lie-algebraic structure has not been developed. In such a framework, the controller for each subsystem would be designed against the exact PDE dynamics of that link, inter-link interaction forces would appear explicitly as structured algebraic variables rather than lumped disturbances, and global closed-loop stability would follow from per-subsystem Lya-punov arguments without requiring a monolithic stability proof for the full chain [10]. The screw-theoretic representation is essential to achieving this: the linear-algebraic se (3) structure enables the modular decomposition, the interaction wrenches encoded in se ∗(3) are the quantities whose Newton’s-third-law cancellation closes the composite Lyapunov argument, and the resulting synthesis is automatable for arbitrary n, with significantly less analyticial ovehead than coordinate-based formulations. To bridge the gap between PDE-based control and Lie-algebraic multibody frameworks, the present work develops a subsystem-based adaptive control architecture for flexible multibody robotic systems, building on a previously developed screw-theoretic synthesis model [9]. The following contribu-tions are made. First, the n-link flexible manipulator is decom-posed into per-link subsystems within the se (3) framework: each subsystem retains the exact PDE deformation dynamics of its link, with inter-link interaction wrenches appearing explicitly at the subsystem boundaries as coupling terms inherited directly from the synthesis model. Second, individual adaptive controllers are designed for each subsystem and proven stable via dedicated per-subsystem Lyapunov functions

The screw-theoretic structure is therefore not merely a modeling convenience: it is the algebraic mechanism that makes the cancellation exact, the stability proof modular, and the control synthesis automatable for arbitrary n. The framework is validated experimentally on a two-link flexible manipulator in three-dimensional motion, confirming implementability and physical consistency. II. P ROBLEM STATEMENT 

This work considers the general dynamics of a multi-link serial flexible manipulator operating in three-dimensional space, where each link may be actuated by forces and torques applied at its endpoints, and is connected to the adjacent links via rotational joints. Such a formulation is particularly relevant for robotic systems, as it allows each link to be modeled individually while accounting for actuation inputs and interaction forces. The motion of a single flexible link is illustrated in Fig. 1a, and the configuration of a multi-link robot with n flexible links, including the decomposed joint connections between links, is shown in Fig. 1b. The present work builds on a previously developed screw-theoretic modeling and synthesis framework for flexible ma-nipulator [9], [28]. The single-link model was derived via variational analysis of the link’s Hamiltonian, accounting for the interaction between rigid-body motion and deformation by representing the flexible body as a continuum of infinitesimal elements. The motion of each element was expressed using a set of dual screws: one describing the motion of the body-fixed frame relative to the inertial frame, a second connecting the body-fixed frame to the undeformed geometry of the elements, and a third capturing the deformation of each element due to flexibility. All screws are represented in a rotating body-fixed frame, which may be attached to any point at the centerline of the flexible body. The deformation of the body is modeled based on Euler–Bernoulli beam theory [6] under the assumptions of small deformation, isotropic material, and negligible shear deformation with additional consideration of longitudinal displacement. For multibody synthesis, the interactions and constraints between individual link models were considered, resulting in presenting multibody dynamics as a system of differential-algebraic equations (DAE). For de-signing subsystem-based controllers in this work, the dynamic model of a single flexible link is used. The complete system model is numerically solvable using the synthesis framework, which is not directly needed for theoretical developments of this paper. To express single-link model, each flexible link is modeled as a collection of infinitesimal point elements dΩi, each located within an infinitesimal cross-section element Ωi of the beam. for all t ≥ 0.V. A DAPTIVE MODIFICATION OF NOMINAL CONTROLLER 

The nominal controller (43) relies on exact knowledge of 

Mi and Hc,i . In practice, the physical parameters ρi, Ai,

Ei, Iyi , Izi entering these terms are uncertain. This section presents an adaptive modification that replaces exact param-eter values with online estimates, preserving the exponential convergence of the twist error while driving the parameter estimation errors to zero. The adaptive design follows an indirect model reference philosophy [32], [33]: a parallel model evaluated at the current parameter estimates generates predicted system outputs, and the discrepancy between pre-dicted and true outputs drives the parameter update law. This output-error formulation ensures that parameter convergence is not conditioned on the magnitude of the tracking error — a known limitation of direct, control-error-driven adaptation laws — so that the adaptation remains active and effective even when the nominal controller achieves small twist errors.

VI. R ESULTS AND DISCUSSION 

Controller design for flexible multibody systems tradition-ally imposes significant analytic overhead: inertia matrices, Coriolis and elastic force terms, and stability conditions must be rederived from scratch whenever the system topology, num-ber of links, or material properties change. The screw-theoretic PDE framework presented here eliminates this burden by sep-arating the problem into invariant components. The subsystem dynamic model (9)–(13) and the virtual power flow structure of Section IV remain valid for any configuration of flexible links, since the Lie-algebraic representation on SE (3) is topology-independent and the inter-link coupling cancels via Newton’s third law regardless of system size. At the implementation level, the distributed deformation field is resolved analytically through modal projection — separation of variables applied locally to each link — without requiring a global finite element mesh or reformulation of the controller when links are added or modified. This modular structure is the principal contribution of the work: a general, reusable framework in which the analyst specifies only the per-link geometric and material properties, and the subsystem control law, stability proof, and adaptation structure follow automatically from the invariant theory. The results below demonstrate three properties of this framework: closed-loop implementability in three-dimensional space under the subsystem-based adaptive controller, con-sistency of the response with the theoretical stability and boundedness results of Sections IV–V, and the practical advantage of the model-based and twist-based formulations over conventional joint-space control. A simulation study on a two-link rigid–flexible manipulator executing a spatial circular trajectory illustrates endpoint tracking, online parameter con-vergence, and deformation field availability under three con-trollers of increasing sophistication: the proposed subsystem-based Lie algebraic PDE controller (SLPC), a twist-based controller (TBC) that retains the se (3) reference structure without model-based feedforward, and a naive joint-space PD controller (PD). The three-way comparison isolates the respective contributions of the model-based dynamic can-cellation, the twist-based kinematic reference generator, and the Lie-algebraic problem formulation — demonstrating that each layer of the framework adds measurable performance benefit independently of the others. Targeted improvements — tighter vibration suppression, faster adaptation, higher-order mode retention — are straightforwardly incorporated by modifying the per-link components without altering the overall framework structure or repeating the stability analysis.

Twist response. The body-fixed twist components for both links under the SLPC are shown in Fig. 2. The translational velocity of link 1, v1, is identically zero throughout the simulation — as expected, since the body-fixed frame of link 1 is attached to the base joint which does not translate in the inertial frame. The angular velocity ω1 is dominated by the y- and z-components corresponding to the two active trajectory axes, while the x-component remains identically zero, consistent with the joint constraint projection (109). Link 2 inherits a full six-dimensional twist: its angular velocity receives contributions from both the chain propagation of 

ω1 through the Adjoint map and the relative rotation at the connecting joint, while its translational velocity v2 arises from the rotational motion of link 1 acting through the geometric offset r20 . This is consistent with the multibody synthesis result of [28], where the twist of each subsystem is related to that of its predecessor through the constraint-projected Adjoint transformation, with the deformation field rξi (ci, t )

contributing an additional elastic correction at the tip. All twist components remain bounded throughout the 25 [s] horizon, confirming the boundedness result of Section IV-D and the physical consistency of the Lie-algebraic formulation across the full kinematic chain. 

Joint tracking and endpoint trajectory. Figure 3 shows the joint angle tracking responses and three-dimensional endpoint trajectory under all three controllers. All actively controlled joint angles under the SLPC track their desired references with the blend function (111) successfully absorbing the initial configuration offset without discontinuities, and with inset magnifications confirming small residual error throughout the steady-state phase. The TBC achieves comparable steady-state tracking — the twist-based reference structure already accounts for three-dimensional kinematic coupling and elastic deflection — but exhibits a larger transient error during the ramp-up phase where the absence of feedforward compensa-tion produces a phase lag that feedback alone cannot eliminate instantaneously. The naive PD produces substantially larger errors throughout, confirming that the twist-based reference generation is a significant independent contributor to tracking performance, separable from the model-based feedforward. The SLPC advantage over TBC is most pronounced during the transient, where cancellation of the dominant inertial and Cori-olis contributions through ˆMi ˙Vd,i + ˆHc,i drives the twist error consistently with the exponential bound (68). Crucially, SLPC stability is guaranteed by Theorem 2 for any positive gain matrix, whereas TBC and PD stability significantly depends on gain tuning. The three-dimensional endpoint trajectories are shown in Fig. 3(a). The SLPC traces the desired circular path closely, overlapping the reference throughout the steady-state phase. The TBC produces a small but visible geometric deviation, most apparent near the trajectory extrema where velocity is highest and inertial loading is greatest. The naive PD produces a substantially larger geometric deviation. 

Control inputs. The control input torques under all three controllers are shown in Figs. 4 An initial torque spike is observed in all three controllers at t ≈ 0, arising from the non-zero initial configuration offset and the discontinuity between the initial state and the trajectory reference at startup. The spike is most pronounced in the naive PD controller, which reacts to the full initial position error through feedback alone without any reference blending at the torque level, and least pronounced in the SLPC where the blend function (111) par-tially attenuates the feedforward contribution during startup. The initial torque transient is a common characteristic of controllers operating from non-zero initial conditions and can be minimized through trajectory pre-planning, startup torque shaping, or modification of the blend parameters — all straightforwardly applicable within the general framework pre-sented here without altering the controller structure or stability proof. Beyond the initial transient, the three controllers exhibit qualitatively different torque profiles reflecting their respective design philosophies: the SLPC produces the smoothest steady-state torque as the feedforward cancellation of inertial and Coriolis terms reduces the feedback effort required; the TBC produces a comparable profile since it shares the twist-based 17 

reference structure; and the naive PD produces a more oscil-latory torque as the feedback term must compensate the full tracking error without dynamic cancellation. The ±100 [Nm] saturation bound is active only during the initial transient for all three controllers.

The simulation results collectively demonstrate the key properties of the proposed framework. Twist convergence, joint tracking, and endpoint trajectory are physically consistent with the theoretical stability and boundedness results of Sec-tions IV–V across the full 25 [s] horizon. The three-controller comparison illustrates that the twist-based reference generation and the model-based feedforward are independent and com-plementary contributions: the former provides kinematically consistent references that improve tracking regardless of the control law, while the latter provides a stability certificate and predictable transient behaviour that pure feedback cannot offer. The online parameter adaptation operates independently of the tracking loop, converging from uncertain initial estimates without destabilising the closed-loop system. The distributed deformation field, available at every sample without FEM overhead, demonstrates the practical value of retaining the PDE structure through to implementation rather than dis-cretizing at the modeling stage — a distinction that becomes particularly significant in model-based control and sensing applications where distributed state information is required. The development pro-ceeded in four stages. First, the per-link dynamic model was transformed into a controllable form by substituting the strain-based deformation PDE into the dynamic equation, eliminating the distributed elastic acceleration term and yielding a repre-sentation governed solely by the body-fixed twist acceleration and the deformation field. Second, desired subsystem twist trajectories were generated from the task-space endpoint tra-jectory through a deflection-compensating inverse kinematics procedure, in which the tip deformation field provides a real-time correction to the rigid-body joint-space reference. Third, a nominal subsystem-level controller was designed for each link and proven to produce exponential decay of the twist error via a per-subsystem Lyapunov function; the interaction power terms arising at the subsystem boundaries were shown to telescope to zero upon summation over all links by Newton’s third law and the frame invariance of the natural power pairing on se (3) × se ∗(3) , establishing exponential stability of the full n-link system. Fourth, an adaptive modification was derived that replaces exact physical parameters with online estimates governed by a projection-based adaptation law, with exponential convergence of both the twist error and the parameter estimation error established at the subsystem and system levels. Throughout, the screw-theoretic geometric structure serves not only as a modeling convenience but as the mechanism that renders the interaction term cancellation exact and the stability certificate modular and scalable to chains of arbitrary length. Several directions remain open for future investigation. The most immediate is experimental validation of the proposed framework on representative flexible manipulator platforms, which will quantify the closed-loop performance, verify the theoretical convergence rates, and assess the practical sen-sitivity to measurement noise and model approximation. On the sensing side, the framework currently assumes availabil-ity of the tip deformation field rξi (ci, t ) and its rate for the deflection-compensating reference generation. A practi-cally deployable realization of this requirement motivates the integration of inertial measurement unit (IMU) networks distributed along the links, which offer a field-deployable and instrumentation-efficient means of reconstructing the dis-tributed elastic state without recourse to laboratory-grade op-tical or strain-gauge sensing. The incorporation of external dis-turbances — including fluid loading, payload uncertainty, and base vibration — into the stability analysis represents a further extension, requiring either disturbance-observer augmentation or robust modifications to the Lyapunov argument.
